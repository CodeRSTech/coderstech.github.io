<p class="text-white-50 mb-4">Complete teardown, solvent flush, and planetary gear rebuild after a 5-minute deep water submersion.</p>

<img src="/assets/images/posts/disassembly.png" class="img-fluid rounded mb-3" alt="Water damaged internals">

**The Incident & Emergency Triage**
Time is the enemy when power tools go for a swim. After a full five minutes submerged in two feet of water, this single-speed Makita folding screwdriver was immediately pulled and disassembled. The initial triage involved wiping away the standing water and applying a generous coat of WD-40 to all components to displace moisture from the motor windings and trigger assembly. The parts were then temporarily sealed in a bag with silica gel packets to draw out ambient moisture.

<img src="/assets/images/posts/battery_housing_and_pcb.png" class="img-fluid rounded mb-3" alt="PCB and Battery Housing">

**The Deep Clean**
While WD-40 is excellent for emergency water displacement, it acts as a solvent that destroys the factory lithium grease necessary for the planetary gears. The "rescue" was quickly escalated to a bare-metal restoration by flushing the printed circuit board (PCB), trigger contacts, and metal gear assemblies with 99% Isopropyl Alcohol. This stripped away the oily WD-40 residue and lingering mineral deposits, leaving a dry, clean surface ready for new lubrication. 

<img src="/assets/images/posts/internal_gears.png" class="img-fluid rounded mb-3" alt="Internal Planetary Gears">

**The Rebuild & Mechanical Calibration**
The re-lubrication phase utilized a plastic-safe Michelin Heavy Duty White Lithium grease, applied meticulously to the metal planetary gears and the primary folding hinge, while strictly avoiding the electric motor housing to prevent brush contamination. 

During reassembly, the automatic spindle lock mechanism—consisting of three small metal rollers seating onto a triangular cam—was re-seated using the new grease as a temporary adhesive to hold them in place. Finally, to prevent torque shear, the plastic motor mounting plate was secured using a back-threading technique to ensure the screws bit into their original threads without stripping the housing. The tool is now fully operational with zero alignment play.
